May 2026
Quantitative Finance

Portfolio Optimization

Mean-variance portfolio optimization and Fama–French (plus momentum) factor analysis across U.S. mutual funds, smart beta ETFs, and hedge fund indices, separating alpha from factor exposure and testing six constrained optimizers in- vs out-of-sample.

Each fund’s excess returns are regressed on MKTRF, SMB, HML, UMD, RMW, and CMA. Portfolios include long-only and long–short global minimum variance and max-Sharpe, a tracking-error-capped max-Sharpe versus equal weight, and a factor-neutral max-Sharpe (±200% per asset). Inputs are estimated in-sample (Jan 2010–Dec 2019) and evaluated out-of-sample (Jan 2020–Dec 2024) without re-estimation; reported statistics are annualized.

A consistent takeaway: unconstrained max-Sharpe and long-short designs overfit in-sample; GMV and tracking-error-bounded strategies tend to generalize better out-of-sample because they rely less on hard-to-estimate expected returns. Hedge fund indices show the most stable in-sample to out-of-sample transfer among the three universes.

Fund return data is bundled in the repo with permission; factor data draws on Kenneth R. French’s library. Data-quality work (e.g. correcting a bad monthly return for FSPHX) is documented in the notebook. Completed as part of the Master of Financial Risk Management (MFRM) program, Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto.
